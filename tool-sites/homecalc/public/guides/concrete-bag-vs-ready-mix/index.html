Concrete guide

Bagged concrete or ready-mix?

Bagged concrete is convenient for small jobs. Ready-mix can become more practical when the bag count, mixing time, and pour timing get too large.

The decision is not just price per cubic foot

Bagged concrete is easy to buy, store, and mix in small batches. It works well for fence posts, small pads, repairs, and projects where delivery access is difficult. The tradeoff is labor: many bags require lifting, mixing, water control, and fast placement.

Ready-mix concrete may cost more upfront or have delivery minimums, but it can save time and reduce consistency problems on larger pours. If your calculator result shows dozens of bags, compare delivery options before assuming bags are cheaper.

When bags become awkward

There is no universal cutoff, but the warning signs are clear: the bag count is high, the pour must be finished quickly, the slab surface needs consistency, or you cannot physically mix and place material fast enough. At that point, the cheapest bag price may not be the cheapest project.

  • Calculate total bags and total weight before buying.
  • Estimate how many batches you can mix per hour.
  • Confirm water access, mixing tools, and helpers.
  • Compare delivery cost and minimum order size.
  • Plan disposal and cleanup before the pour starts.
Practical rule: use the calculator to get volume first, then price both bagged and delivered options when the bag count starts to feel like a full day of mixing.
