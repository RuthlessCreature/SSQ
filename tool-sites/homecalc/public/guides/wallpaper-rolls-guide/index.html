Wallpaper guide

How to think about wallpaper roll coverage.

Wallpaper ordering is not just wall area divided by roll area. Repeat, trimming, batch, and wall layout can all change the final roll count.

Roll coverage is a starting point

A roll may list a theoretical square footage, but usable coverage can be lower after trimming, matching, and cutting around doors or windows. The difference is small for plain wallpaper and larger for patterns with a long vertical repeat.

When a seller provides a roll calculator or usable coverage after repeat matching, use that figure. If only theoretical coverage is listed, add a waste allowance before rounding up to full rolls.

FactorEffect on rollsPlanning note
Large repeatMore offcuts.Use a higher waste allowance.
Many short wallsMore starts and stops.Can waste more than one long wall.
OpeningsReduce area but still require cutting.Subtract large openings cautiously.
Batch numberAffects color match.Order enough from the same batch.

Why ordering one extra roll can be rational

If a wallpaper is special order, discounted, or likely to sell out, an extra roll can protect the project. A future repair or miscut may be impossible to match if the batch changes. This is similar to keeping spare tile from the same dye lot.

Before ordering

  • Measure each wall receiving wallpaper.
  • Confirm roll width, roll length, and usable coverage.
  • Check repeat type and match instructions.
  • Verify whether unopened rolls can be returned.
  • Keep labels until installation is complete.
Buying tip: do not mix batches unless the seller confirms they match. Small color shifts can become obvious across adjacent strips.
