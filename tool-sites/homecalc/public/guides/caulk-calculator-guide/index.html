Supplies guide

How to plan caulk tubes for trim and seams.

Caulk quantity depends on joint length, bead size, surface gaps, and whether the joint is paintable or wet-area rated.

Measure joint length first

For baseboards, measure the top edge where trim meets the wall and any vertical seams that need filling. For bathrooms and kitchens, measure tubs, counters, backsplashes, sinks, and corners separately. Different joints may need different products, so do not combine them blindly.

Tube coverage varies by bead size. A thin bead covers much more length than a large gap. If a joint is wide or deep, backer rod may be needed before caulk so the bead has the right shape.

Common buying mistakes

  • Buying non-paintable caulk for painted trim.
  • Using interior trim caulk in wet areas.
  • Underestimating gaps on old walls.
  • Forgetting a caulk gun, scraper, blades, or cleanup supplies.
  • Opening too many tubes before the surface is ready.
Planning note: caulk is small in cost but high in finish impact. Buy the right type for the joint rather than using one tube for every condition.
