Plan matching tile stock and repair spares

Buy enough stock to finish the room, then decide how much matching material to keep for repairs.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Ask what can be supplied together

Record the exact product, format, finish, carton coverage and whatever shade, caliber or lot codes the supplier uses. Take a photo of the carton label for your own records. Two boxes with the same style name can carry different production identifiers. Ask the supplier which markings matter for the selected line and whether the full order is available together.

Keep small format trim and accent pieces on their own lines. A box of field tile does not cover a trim profile that must be ordered by piece. If the edging method changes, revise the separate line and check the planned sharing of cut pieces before changing the field tile order.

Distinguish a projected reserve from a physical spare

Suppose a worksheet returns five cartons at 15.49 sq ft each, with 77.45 sq ft purchased against a 62.30 sq ft target. The paper difference is 15.15 sq ft. That could end up as an unopened box, several usable full pieces or offcuts with limited repair value. Inventory remains uncertain until the job is complete.

Three different counts to record
WhenCountWhy it matters
At orderWhole cartons boughtDetermines cash and supplied stock
After installationFull pieces, usable cuts and sealed cartonsDetermines likely repair and return options
After return decisionPractical reserve by format and shadeReduces uncertainty for a future repair

If the job needs at least one extra unopened carton reserved regardless of the cuts, make it an explicit add-on. With five project cartons, the guaranteed spare makes six. Record the cost of the spare as an inventory choice. If the style is readily available and the batch risk is low, a smaller reserve may be acceptable to the owner.

Write the reserve into the project handover

Label spare stock with the room, product and production information. Record where it is stored and whether the pieces are full stock or offcuts. Keep the field carton estimate and the return terms with that record so the next buyer can identify the original order.
