Measure sloped ceilings and gable walls for paint

A ceiling can cover more area than the floor below it. Measure the sloped faces and keep their coating separate.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Identify which surfaces actually receive each finish

Consider a room with a symmetric gable, 12 ft across and 14 ft along the ridge. The ridge rises 3 ft above the wall tops. Each ceiling face has a 6 ft horizontal run. Its sloped length is the square root of 6 squared plus 3 squared, about 6.708 ft. The two ceiling faces total about 187.83 sq ft, compared with the floor's 168 sq ft.

These figures assume two planar faces with an uninterrupted ridge. The sketch is only a geometric example. If the shape has different slopes, dormers or light wells, measure separate faces. Use reliably available plans or measurements and trained help where access is unsafe.

One coat on the ceiling at an illustrative 350 sq ft per gallon needs about 0.54 gal. Two coats need about 1.07 gal. Using 168 sq ft of floor area would predict 0.96 gal for two coats and make one gallon appear sufficient. The same geometry may change another product if you count the gable ends with their wall finish.

Ask the supplier whether the product's coverage range applies to the texture present. If beams, skylight reveals or boxed soffits receive the same paint, include only the faces actually coated and deduct relevant openings. Record those adjustments as separate measured rows rather than applying an unexplained correction to the ceiling total.

Enter the measured surface total in the paint calculator's net area field, and use the coverage and coat count for that surface. Compare package counts using the paint coverage bracket.
