Compare plank directions using cut lengths

Floor area stays the same when planks turn. The row ends, repeat cuts and possible reuse change.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Use two runs from the same sketch

Take a rectangular 14 ft by 10 ft room for illustration. Draw one direction where the planks run 14 ft, and another where they run 10 ft. With nominal 48 in stock, the runs are 168 in or 120 in. Dividing either by 48 leaves 24 in after the third or second whole-plank length respectively. Even when the tail length matches, the number of rows and the locations of cuts will differ.

Make a separate note of doorway positions and where the direction has to continue into another space. Joint staggering, minimum starter or end lengths, locking ends, perimeter clearances, and installation constraints are product-specific. Copy those requirements from the selected installation guide into your drawing before deciding which portions of the arithmetic are usable.

Estimate row counts and keep candidates for offcut reuse

At 7 in nominal width and before edge adjustments, the short cross-span of 120 in suggests 18 rows. Across 168 in, it suggests 24 rows. A 24 in tail length across 24 rows represents more end pieces than across 18. The count is just a prompt to draw the layout. Some head and tail pieces can share stock, while a remainder with the wrong locking edge may be unusable.

If the second layout requires several more rejected ends, track that difference as pieces on the sketch. Compare it with the material already implied by your allowance before increasing the order. A direction with more rows can still use less stock if its remaining pieces fit better. Confirm the final direction and manufacturer instructions before relying on the comparison.
