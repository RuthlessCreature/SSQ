Decide whether to buy a phased floor order together

Combining phases can save a carton. Separate buying can matter when the second room is uncertain.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Calculate both purchase schedules from the same assumptions

Two rooms use the same selected boxed product with 20 sq ft per carton. Phase 1 is 88 sq ft and phase 2 is 82. An illustrative 10% allowance gives 96.8 and 90.2 sq ft. Separately they need five cartons each. Combined they need 187 sq ft, which takes ten cartons too. In these measurements there is no package saving from combining.

Now suppose the second room drops to 74 sq ft when a closet stays outside the scope. With the same assumption, it needs 81.4 sq ft and still rounds to five cartons. The combined 178.2 sq ft fits in nine cartons. The choice is one carton against the terms for holding or reordering stock.

Check whether leftovers can actually cross phases

A combined estimate assumes both areas use the same SKU and that material left from phase 1 can be stored and reused. Record production information and planned storage. Track sealed cartons separately from pieces. When the second phase begins, count usable inventory already on hand and subtract it once, with a separate quote for any shortfall.

If the second phase may be canceled, model the cost of stock that cannot be returned within the stated window. With a $60 carton, combining the smaller plan avoids $60 at the original price if both phases happen. If the second phase falls through and four unopened cartons cannot be returned, carrying unused stock can tie up $240. Ask whether the seller can reserve matching stock or honor a written split delivery.

Record the combined decision

Save both room rows in the purchase worksheet. Keep a dated note on included phases and any guaranteed reserve, and compare delivery using the quote checklist.
