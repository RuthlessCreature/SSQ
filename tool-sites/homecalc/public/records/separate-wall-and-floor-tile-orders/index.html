Separate floor tile, wall tile and edge trim

A bathroom plan can contain a floor field, a wall field and a linear edge. Give each product its own buying unit.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Draw the finish boundary for each product

In this illustrative scope, the floor receives one SKU across 46 sq ft. A dry-area half-height wall gets a separate SKU along an 11 ft run at a height of 4 ft. A 2 ft by 1.5 ft access panel is excluded from the wall finish. The wall field is 44 - 3 = 41 sq ft. The top edge needs 11 ft of a specified profile, assuming no interruptions or returns.

The access panel is a deduction from the vertical finish only. It does not change the floor. Mark inside returns and exposed ends if the wall design calls for them. Give a niche, accent strip or other tile size a separate row. For a wet area, use a project-specific system specification before making an order.

Ten cartons in total tells the buyer how many packages to collect, but no carton can be moved between these tile lines. Two 8 ft lengths cover the total on paper. If the design requires one continuous profile, verify that an 11 ft or longer piece can be sourced and transported. Ask the installer to confirm the detailing.

Inventory may create a different purchasing decision for each line. A shade mismatch on the wall is a separate supplier issue from floor carton coverage. Keep the visual approvals, batch codes, lead times, return terms, and approved substitutions with their own line items.

Save separate worksheets

Run the floor and wall as two projects in the carton worksheet. Keep each allowance and price on its own export. Record the profile length on the separate linear-stock schedule.
