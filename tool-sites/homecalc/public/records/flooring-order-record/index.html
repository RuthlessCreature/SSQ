Worked example | Updated November 26, 2026

Connected rooms should be measured separately before they are ordered together.

This fictional flooring record combines a bedroom, a closet, and a short hall. It demonstrates why an honest order starts with distinct areas and a stated plank direction rather than a single house-wide square-foot number.

Scenario: bedroom, closet, and hallFinish scope: continuous flooringOutput: whole cartons

1. Keep each connected space on the measurement sheet

The planning scope calls for one continuous material across three spaces. The closet is included because flooring will be installed inside it. The hall is recorded independently because it narrows and creates cuts that a large rectangular-room shortcut would hide. Existing cabinetry, stairs, and areas receiving another finish are outside this sample scope.

Raw material area192.5 sq ftNo allowance or carton rounding yet

The raw total is deliberately modest. It is not inflated until the layout decision has been made. That separation makes it possible to revisit the hallway or closet without arguing about a hidden percentage.

2. Explain why the waste allowance is 10%

The assumed planks run in the long direction of the bedroom and continue into the hall. The room is mostly rectangular, but the closet opening and hall transitions interrupt several courses. The record applies 10% for end cuts and practical selection: 192.5 x 1.10 = 211.75 sq ft. This is a planning allowance, not a guarantee of installed waste.

Two changes would force a new decision. A diagonal or herringbone layout can create materially different offcuts, while an unusually long plank or a complex transition plan can reduce the usefulness of short offcuts. In either case, it would be misleading to reuse this 10% assumption merely because the floor area stayed the same.

Direction is an input: write it on the sketch before entering a waste percentage. The same rooms can produce different cut patterns when the boards turn ninety degrees.

3. Round by the product carton, then decide on repair stock

Assume the selected product supplies 22.4 sq ft per carton. The adjusted result is 211.75 / 22.4 = 9.45 cartons, so ten cartons are required to cover the calculation. Ten cartons supply 224 sq ft, leaving 12.25 sq ft above the planning figure. That remaining quantity might be useful for a repair, but the record does not describe it as a mandated reserve until the product's availability, matching risk, and return policy are known.

Raw area192.5 sq ft
Layout allowance10% = 19.25 sq ft
Adjusted quantity211.75 sq ft
Carton coverage22.4 sq ft
Purchase quantity10 cartons = 224 sq ft

The carton result is larger than the formula result because a carton is indivisible. Do not work backwards and label the entire difference as waste; some portion may remain unused, may be returned, or may be intentionally retained after checking whether exact replacements will be obtainable later.

4. Order checklist for this record

  • Confirm the carton coverage and actual plank dimensions on the selected product, not a similar product listing.
  • Record whether the same material continues through every doorway or stops at a transition.
  • Verify that closets, alcoves, and under-furniture areas are included or excluded intentionally.
  • Keep all cartons from the same order identifiable until the installation plan and return decision are complete.
  • Separate the flooring quantity from underlayment, transitions, moisture management, trim, fasteners, and any required system materials.

A flooring estimate cannot determine substrate preparation, expansion requirements, moisture conditions, or installation compatibility. Those questions belong to the product instructions and the project-specific review, not to a square-foot calculator.

The difference is only 0.45 sq ft across the order, yet the higher nominal carton price produces the lower subtotal at the second allowance. Record that threshold and confirm that the exact product you priced matches the product on the carton.

On the connected plan, count the bedroom length from wall to the chosen transition line. Start the hall at that same line so the doorway strip is counted once. If the closet uses a separate product, remove its 12.5 sq ft from this order and compute another purchase line. Rounding each zone independently would overstate the order when unopened cartons can be shared.
