Combine paint only when the product matches

Group surfaces by the product that actually goes into the cart. Round each line to available containers.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Give each finish its own line

For this constructed job, the entry needs 260 sq ft of a base-color wall finish, an alcove needs 84 sq ft of an accent finish, and patches totaling 42 sq ft may need a separate primer. At two finish coats and 350 sq ft per gallon, the wall lines need about 1.486 and 0.480 gallons. The 42 sq ft of primer takes 0.168 gallons if the selected label says 250 sq ft per gallon for that use.

Choose the product and confirm the coating system before combining any of the lines. A primer purchased separately has a different purpose and may have its own coverage. A partial accent can need a separate purchase even when the paint type and sheen match the larger wall line.

At a hypothetical $42 per gallon and $16 per quart for the base finish, two gallons cost $84. A gallon and two quarts cost $74 and supply 1.5 gallons. That leaves only about 0.014 gallons above the base estimate. If the budget includes a 0.25-gallon reserve for that color, the need rises to about 1.736 gallons, so the cheaper package combination must be changed.

Do the same comparison for each finish. Record compatible leftovers only after confirming the label, condition and usable quantity. Product, color, sheen and tint compatibility matter before relying on a can from a previous project. Ask the supplier about return options for tinted containers before assuming that an unused can can be credited.

Bring a decision sheet to the counter

Bring the area, number of coats, coverage rate, theoretical volume, package choices and desired spare for each row. Use the paint calculator to estimate one finish at a time. The coverage bracket guide shows how to test the pack decision when the label lists a range.
