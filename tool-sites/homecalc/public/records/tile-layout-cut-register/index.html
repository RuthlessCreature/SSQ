Check tile cuts before setting a waste allowance

A cut register shows where a small room can use more material than its area suggests.

HomeCalc Tools | November 26, 2026

Mark a repeatable reference line

Sketch the boundary and draw the tile module to scale using the measured tile and planned joint. Mark the cuts at the doorway, vanity and far wall. Compare two starting lines before selecting an allowance. Keep separate rows for uncut pieces, pieces that need one cut, and pieces with a notch or a visible edge.

For an arithmetic example, take a 58 in clear span before perimeter deductions and a simplified 12 in planning module. Four full modules leave 10 in. Putting all 10 in at one end is a different cut plan from centering four modules and leaving about 5 in at each end. Your actual dimensions, joint layout and perimeter requirements determine the final cut sizes. Use the module only to compare alternatives at sketch stage.

Reserve a piece until its reuse is verified

For each repeated cut, record its dimensions, quantity, which factory edges must remain, and a location that could use the remainder. A 5 in cut from nominal 12 in stock can appear to produce another 7 in piece. Cutting loss, visible edges, pattern direction and color variation can disqualify that remainder. Assign it to an actual location before subtracting it from the order.

At 70 sq ft net and 14.8 sq ft per carton, five cartons cover 74 sq ft and six cover 88.8 sq ft. Five boxes allow just 5.71% above the measured area. A 6% target needs 74.2 sq ft, so it already exceeds five cartons. Record how close the estimate is to that step and keep the cut count as a separate check. Do not count the same notched pieces both in a dedicated reserve and in a blanket allowance.

Check with the selected carton

Use the supplier's exact size, finish and batch details for a layout-dependent quote. If the product changes, redraw the most constrained edge and check the carton count again. Apply the measured area and revised allowance in the purchase worksheet or load the bathroom comparison.
