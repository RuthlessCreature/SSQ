Paint estimate

How this paint estimate is calculated

The calculator starts with wall area instead of floor area. For a rectangular room, wall area is the room perimeter multiplied by wall height. Door and window openings are subtracted, then the remaining paintable area is multiplied by the number of coats. The final gallon estimate is rounded up to whole buckets because most stores do not sell partial buckets.

Formula: paintable wall area = 2 x (length + width) x wall height - doors and windows. Coverage to buy for = paintable wall area x coats. Gallons = coverage to buy for / coverage per gallon.

Example: 12 ft by 10 ft room

A 12 ft by 10 ft room with 8 ft walls has a perimeter of 44 ft. Multiplying by 8 ft gives 352 sq ft of wall surface. If the doors and windows total 45 sq ft, the paintable wall area is 307 sq ft. With two coats, the room needs coverage for 614 sq ft.

If the chosen paint covers 350 sq ft per gallon, the estimate is 1.75 gallons. A cautious purchase would be two 1-gallon buckets, or one larger container if the store sells the exact size you need.

When to round up

  • Fresh drywall, skim-coated areas, or many wall repairs.
  • Textured walls, brick, rough plaster, or porous surfaces.
  • Strong color changes, especially light over dark or dark over light.
  • Rooms where future touch-ups need an exact color match.
  • Projects where primer is skipped or rolled thinly.
Planning note: This tool estimates wall paint only. It does not include ceilings, trim, cabinets, primer, brushes, rollers, tape, or drop cloths unless you add those costs separately.

Field workflow

Turn a calculator result into a reliable paint order

Paint estimates become useful only when the measured surface, product coverage, and number of coats describe the same job. Treat the result as a buying range, then use the paint label to choose the conservative end of that range.

Measure before you price

  1. Decide exactly what will receive the finish coat. Measure wall faces separately from ceilings, trim, doors, cabinets, and accent walls. A room can use several paint products, so one room dimension does not always equal one paint order.
  2. Walk the perimeter with a tape measure and write down each wall length and the wall height. For a room with a bulkhead, a sloped ceiling, or a partial wall, calculate that section separately instead of forcing it into a rectangular-room shortcut.
  3. Subtract large openings only after you have recorded them. A standard door or a wide window reduces wall area, but the cuts around those openings still use roller loading, edging paint, and some time.
  4. Read the selected product label before entering coverage. Coverage is usually a range because porous drywall, texture, repairs, and strong color changes use more paint than a smooth wall in a similar color.
  5. Choose coats based on the job rather than wishful thinking. A repaint close to the existing color may cover in two finish coats; primer, patching, red-to-white changes, and saturated colors can make the total system different.

Worked planning scenario

Imagine a bedroom with two long walls, two short walls, one door, and two windows. Measure the four wall faces first, then subtract only the door and window rectangles. Multiply the remaining area by the planned number of finish coats. If the answer is near the next container size, choose the larger size and label any reserve paint with room, color, sheen, and date. That reserve is often more valuable than a small apparent saving when a repaired wall needs an exact touch-up months later.

How to read the result

A gallon result is not a promise that every surface will consume that exact amount. It is a comparison tool. If two paints have different coverage ranges, compare both at the lower stated coverage. If the calculated total says 1.9 gallons and the product comes in one-gallon cans, two gallons is the honest purchase quantity; do not pretend a 0.1-gallon margin will survive roller tray waste, cutting in, or a porous repair.

Before checkout

  • Confirm whether ceilings, trim, closets, doors, and accent walls need separate paint products.
  • Check the label for the actual coverage range, recommended roller nap, and recoat time.
  • Include primer, patch material, caulk, tape, roller covers, tray liners, and cleaning supplies in the project budget.
  • Keep all finish paint from a single room in the same sheen and, when practical, the same batch.
  • Do a small test patch before committing to a color change or a premium container size.
Scope boundary: This calculator intentionally estimates wall finish paint rather than prescribing a coating system. It cannot determine whether stains need a specialty primer, whether moisture damage has been fixed, or whether lead-safe work practices are required. Resolve those conditions before treating a quantity estimate as a shopping list.

Use the estimate well

Keep the paint calculator connected to the real job

A useful calculator is a record of a specific room, material, layout, and date. Write down the measurements, product package size, coverage or yield, and any allowance you selected. Without that record, a number copied into a shopping cart becomes hard to check when a wall changes, a carton has different coverage, or the selected product is out of stock.

The result should be revised whenever the assumptions change. That is not a failure of the original calculation; it is how an early planning estimate stays honest. Keep the first version, note what changed, and compare the new purchase quantity with the exact package sizes and return policy before ordering.

Recheck the estimate when any of these change

  • The measured surface gains or loses an opening, alcove, border, closet, or separate material zone.
  • The layout changes direction, pattern, joint spacing, strip height, board length, or finished termination point.
  • The product package, usable coverage, yield, repeat, actual dimension, or installation instructions differ from the first assumption.
  • The site inspection reveals repairs, uneven surfaces, moisture, access limits, or a condition that adds preparation or waste.
  • The estimate sits close to the next carton, roll, panel, bucket, or stock-length boundary.

Three questions before you place an order

What number should I bring to the store?

Bring the raw measured quantity, the allowance and why you chose it, the adjusted quantity, and the final number of purchasable units. A store associate or installer can help check a transparent estimate; they cannot reliably check a single unexplained total.

Should I always buy extra?

Buy a reasoned reserve for cuts, package rounding, defects, and repairs, not an arbitrary pile of material. The value of same-batch spare material is higher when a product is special order, patterned, dyed, discontinued, or difficult to match.

When should I stop and ask for help?

Pause when the work involves structural loading, waterproofing, drainage, electrical systems, plumbing, fire ratings, permits, hazardous materials, or an active building failure. Material quantity is not a substitute for a safe scope of work.
